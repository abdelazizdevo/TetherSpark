A few things to keep in mind

IPv4 TCP and UDP are relayed; IPv6 is not tunneled. Android allows one active VPN at a time. Stop sharing before unplugging the cable.

Let’s find the missing link.

Five quick checks to get things flowing again.

Run checks for a fresh look at your connection.
01

No device / cable check

Check your cable and USB port.

CHECK
Try a data-capable cable and another USB port. Keep your phone unlocked. If Windows still cannot see it, install your phone manufacturer’s USB driver.
02

USB debugging authorization

Your phone needs to trust this PC.

CHECK
Enable USB debugging in Developer options, then accept the RSA prompt on your phone. If the prompt is missing, revoke USB debugging authorizations on the phone and reconnect.
03

VPN approval window

Two minutes to approve Android’s VPN request.

CHECK
Unlock your phone after starting a session and approve the VPN connection. Stop another Android VPN first. If the request times out, start sharing again to retry.
04

Port 31416 conflict

One local relay at a time.

CHECK
Close another Gnirehtet or reverse-tethering session using port 31416. Check Activity log for the exact startup error, then retry.
05

Connected without internet

Check PC internet, firewall, and DNS.

MANUAL
Open a website on your PC, allow the relay through your firewall, and check your DNS settings. To test the USB path, temporarily turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data on your phone and open a website.
